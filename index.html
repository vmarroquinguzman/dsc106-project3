02 / DATA & LIMITATIONS

What are we measuring?

NASA MODIS active-fire detections, filtered to vegetation-type detections in the study area, aggregated by year, month, and approximate California region. A detection is an observed thermal anomaly, not a unique wildfire or an estimate of burned area.

Regions are assigned using latitude bands in the exploratory notebook. These are approximate geographic groupings, not official California regional boundaries. Satellite coverage and detection conditions can also affect the counts.

Before submission: confirm the latitude cutoffs and the California boundary filter against the exploratory notebook, and document both here. If records were only filtered by a rectangular coordinate box, do not describe the results as strictly within California until that is corrected.

03 / DESIGN & DEVELOPMENT

Why this design?

Encodings: month is encoded by horizontal position and average percentage by vertical position on shared scales. Small multiples separate regions, avoiding confusing overlapping lines. Solid lines represent selected years and dashed lines provide the same historical reference in every panel.

Interaction: a D3 brush selects contiguous years, updating all three panels. This supports comparing early, recent, and individual-year patterns without changing the question or visual encoding.

Alternatives: we considered a spatial fire map, but selected linked seasonal charts because the question is about timing rather than individual fire locations. Annual percentage normalization avoids letting high-detection years dominate averages.
